Field guide

Everything on screen is derived from the seed phrase — the terrain, the weather, the names, the history. Nothing is pre-authored.

What you are looking at

A world built in four passes: plates are seeded and drifted, their collisions raise crust, rivers erode it, then wind carries moisture inland and rains it out. Everything downstream — biomes, habitability, where cities appear — follows from that.

Colour
Biome, decided per-pixel from local temperature and rainfall. Change the climate and the colours move with it.
Purple entries
News from another planet in this system. Its full history is waiting there if you travel.
Blue threads
Rivers, traced down the real drainage network. Thickness is upstream catchment area.
White
Ice — wherever the surface sits below freezing. Not painted on; it is the temperature model showing through.
Amber specks
City lights, visible only on the night side once a civilisation exists.
Names
Each world invents its own phonology, so its continents, seas, mountains and nations all sound related.

The system

The seed builds a whole star system, not one planet. A star is drawn with a real spectral class, and its luminosity sets where the habitable zone falls. Planets are spaced outward, and what each one is follows from where it landed: molten close in, rock and water in the zone, ice and giants past the snow line.

Planet list
Click any row to generate that world in full and travel to it. Its insolation, tilt and climate come from its actual orbit, so the outer worlds really are frozen.
Orrery
The System view: orbits to scale in order, the star, and the habitable zone drawn as a green band. Click a planet out there to visit it. V toggles.
Giants
Gas and ice giants have no surface. You get banded cloud decks with real differential rotation and a persistent storm instead, and the probe reads cloud-top conditions. Terraforming, uplift and the terrain brushes need rock, so they are greyed out there; an impactor still leaves its mark in the chronicle.
Colonies
A civilisation that reaches its orbital age will launch for another planet. Watch the craft cross in the orrery; the target is marked once they land.
Companions
About one system in five has a second star in a tight orbit. It adds its light to the habitable zone and shows as a second sun close beside the first.
Comets
Long, eccentric orbits drawn in the orrery. When one swings inside about three AU of your world it appears in the sky with a tail, and a close perihelion sometimes ends in a strike.
Moons
Click one to fly to it: a small cratered world with its own name and figures. Orbital-age polities put bases on them. When a moon crosses the star its shadow sweeps the surface and the day goes dim.
In the sky
From any surface the rest of the system is visible where it really is: the star, and the other planets as small discs you can click to travel to. Every world runs at once; the ones you are not watching write only their headlines into the chronicle, tagged with their name.

Genesis

Seed phrase
The whole system in one string. The same phrase always rebuilds the same star, the same planets and the same terrain.
Archetype
Presets for ocean fraction, obliquity, atmosphere and relief. Pelagic drowns the land, Arid strips the water, Glacial dims the star, Infernal floors the greenhouse, Xeno tilts the axis hard.
Mesh resolution
10k / 41k / 164k surface vertices. Ultra is sharper and noticeably slower to build.

Lithosphere

Crustal plates
How many plates the shell is broken into. Fewer plates means larger, blockier continents.
Tectonic vigor
How fast plates move, and so how violently their margins collide.
Orogeny
How much a collision lifts. Turn it up for improbable mountain walls.
Erosion cycles
Rounds of stream-power incision and slope creep. More cycles cut deeper valleys and wider floodplains.
Sea level
Floods or drains the existing terrain instantly. Watch continental shelves appear.
Relief scale
Vertical exaggeration only. It does not change the terrain, just how tall it looks.
Advance drift
Steps the plates forward and rebuilds the crust, morphing between the old world and the new one. Ranges rise, seas open.

The first four rebuild the planet from scratch when you let go of the slider. The last three are instant.

Atmosphere and climate

Insolation
Starlight reaching the planet, relative to 1361 W/m2.
Greenhouse
Degrees added to the whole surface. Drag it and the ice caps advance or retreat live.
Obliquity
Axial tilt, which drives the seasons. Past about 45 degrees the poles get more sun than the equator.
Hydrosphere
How much water evaporates and travels. Low values give continental interiors no rain at all.
Cloud deck
Cloud cover. Purely visual.
Composition
Sets the colour of the sky, the limb and the ambient light.

Surface channels

Natural
Biomes as they would be seen from orbit.
Hypsometry
Height, from ocean trench to summit.
Thermal
Mean surface temperature, -40 to +45 C.
Precipitation
Annual rainfall. Rain shadows behind mountains are the point.
Biome index
Flat biome colour with no shading, for reading boundaries.
Plate tectonics
One colour per plate. Orange margins are converging, cyan are pulling apart.
Political
Territory by polity, with frontiers picked out.
Habitability
Where life and people can actually live. Cities appear at the bright spots.

Intervention

Probe
Read out coordinates, elevation, climate, biome, plate, catchment and owner for any point.
Uplift / Subside
Raise or lower crust inside the brush. Rivers and coastlines re-solve afterwards.
Impactor
Excavates a crater with a raised rim, erases anyone living there, and throws the planet into an impact winter that decays over centuries.
Hotspot
Builds a volcano and vents enough gas to nudge the greenhouse.
Seed life
Waters a region and pushes the biosphere along.
Snowball
Ice-albedo runaway. The oceans freeze from the poles in.
Runaway
Water-vapour runaway. Shelves flood, the tropics sterilise.

Time and the chronicle

Time runs in chronicle years. Life crosses its thresholds in the first thousand or so; the first polity appears after about 1,200 years, once the biosphere can support it. Run at 8x or 60x to get there, then drop back to 1x to watch borders move.

Orbital forcing
Milankovitch wobbles in tilt, insolation and greenhouse. Left on, ice ages arrive by themselves, sea level falls as ice locks up water, and land bridges open. The timeline shows it.
Registry
Living polities with territory, population and technological era \u2014 neolithic through to orbital \u2014 and who rules each one.
Rulers
Every polity has a named sovereign, consul, marshal or premier who reigns, wages the wars, presides over the golden ages, dies, and earns an epithet. Successions are usually quiet; sometimes they are not.
Cinema
Big events pull the camera to them briefly with a title card: impacts with ejecta, launches climbing to orbit, sparks along a war front, an eclipse shadow. The Tour button chains a whole sequence and travels between worlds; it also starts on its own if the terminal is left idle.
Tree of life
Life radiates from the sea into the shore, the forests and plains, the deserts, the ice, the peaks and the air, one lineage descending from another. Ice ages, heat, impacts and snowball events cull it. The first land lineage to reach sapience founds the first polity. The Life channel maps who holds each niche; the codex prints the whole tree.
Chronicle
Written as it happens. Amber entries are civilisations, green are biological, brown geological, red catastrophic.
Timeline
Global mean temperature in amber over an ice-cover band in teal; ticks mark living polities. Drag across it to scrub history: borders and climate rewind to that year. Press play to return to the present.
Wars
Declared, fought along a front that glows red in every channel, and ended by a peace that redraws the border. Trade routes between polities at peace show as teal lanes; roads between a polity's own cities are amber.
Tracking
By default the camera rides with the surface, so a place stays under you while the star and the other planets wheel overhead. Switch to free orbit to hang in space and watch the surface roll past.
Zoom
Detail arrives with distance. Far out: the other planets in the sky, continents and seas. Closer: cities. Closest: satellites in orbit, once someone has launched them. Moons are named and can be clicked.

Performance

The cost here is the surface shader, not the simulation: every pixel of the globe evaluates procedural noise for micro-relief, moisture mottling, surface normals and cloud density. Render quality trades noise octaves and resolution for frame rate, and the renderer also drops its own resolution automatically if frames get slow. Coarse mesh resolution helps world-build time, not frame time.
